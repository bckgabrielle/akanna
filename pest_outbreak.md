# Pest Outbreak, Fall Armyworm and Similar

Keywords: armyworm, holes in leaves, sawdust droppings, chewed whorl, caterpillar, pest, infestation

## Quick checks
Ragged holes in young leaves, especially in the whorl, the tight roll of leaves at the center of the plant.
Sawdust looking droppings sitting inside the whorl.
Open the whorl and look for a caterpillar with an upside down Y mark on its head. That confirms fall armyworm.
Check at least twenty plants spread across the field, not just one corner, since the damage often starts patchy.

## What to do this week
Hand pick and destroy caterpillars in the morning or evening when they are most active.
Put ash, sand, or wet soil directly into the whorl. It suffocates young larvae and costs almost nothing.
A little soap dissolved in water can work against soft bodied young larvae. Test it on a few plants first.
If pesticide is available, spray it in the late afternoon or evening, aimed at the whorl, and stick to the label rate.
Change control methods where you can instead of using the same pesticide every time. Repeating it builds resistance.

## When to get help
If more than a fifth of the plants show fresh damage after your first round of control, call the extension officer. Fall armyworm spreads across farms, so a coordinated response works far better than one farm acting alone.
