# Drought and Water Stress

Keywords: drought, dry spell, wilting, cracked soil, curling leaves, no rain, water stress

## Quick checks
Leaf rolling that recovers by evening is early stress. Curling that stays rolled through the night is severe stress.
Dig fifteen to twenty centimeters near the roots. Dry powder below the surface crust means there is no reserve moisture left.
Compare stressed plants against any sheltered ones nearby, near a tree or a wall, to separate drought from disease.

## What to do this week
Mulch around the base of each plant with dry grass, crop residue, or leaves to cut down evaporation.
If any water is available, give it early morning or evening in a ring around the stem, not overhead.
Pull weeds right away. They are competing for the little moisture left in the soil.
Hold off on nitrogen fertilizer during active drought. It can make wilting worse. Wait for moisture to return.
If you keep animals, give water to breeding and dairy stock first.

## When to get help
If more than a third of the field shows leaves that are dry and brittle rather than just rolled, plan to replant once rain returns instead of trying to save the current stand.
Ask the nearest extension office about drought tolerant seed for the next season.
