# Post Harvest Storage Loss

Keywords: grain storage, weevils, moldy grain, aflatoxin, spoilage, storage pest, sprouting in store

## Quick checks
A musty smell or visible mold points to a moisture problem, not just pests. Moldy grain can carry aflatoxin and should not be eaten or fed to animals.
Small holes in the kernels with fine powder around them mean weevils or grain borers.
Grain that feels warm in the middle of the sack or bin can mean active insect activity or fermentation from too much moisture.

## What to do this week
Dry grain fully before storing it. Kernels should feel hard and break cleanly when bitten, not bend. Dry on a raised mat, never directly on bare ground.
Store in sealed, airtight containers where you can, hermetic bags or clean drums with tight lids. This suffocates most pests without any chemicals.
Move any moldy or heavily infested grain away from the clean stock right away. Do not mix them.
Raise storage containers off the floor and away from the walls to cut down moisture and keep rodents out.
Check stored grain every two or three weeks for the first few months, since that is when infestations are easiest to catch early.

## When to get help
Grain meant for sale or for young children that shows mold should be tested for aflatoxin where testing is available. Do not rely on smell or sight alone when the stakes are that high.
