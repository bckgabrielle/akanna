# Flooding and Waterlogging

Keywords: flood, waterlogged, standing water, root rot, yellowing leaves after rain, drowned crop

## Quick checks
Yellowing that starts from the bottom leaves after heavy rain usually points to roots starved of oxygen, not a nutrient problem.
Water still standing more than a day or two after rain stops means drainage is not enough.
Pull up one plant. Healthy roots are firm and pale. Rotting roots are dark, soft, and smell bad.

## What to do this week
Once it is safe, dig small drainage channels toward the lowest point of the land, even shallow ones cut by hand.
Keep heavy equipment and animals off the wet soil. Walking on it compacts the ground and makes drainage worse later.
Wait to fertilize until the plant shows new growth. Damaged roots cannot take up nutrients well right after flooding.
If a plant has clearly rotted roots and no new growth after a week, pull it out so it does not spread fungal problems to healthy neighbors.
Keep any harvested produce on a raised, dry platform, well away from the flooded area.

## When to get help
Crop loss across the whole field, or the same spot flooding every season, is worth a conversation with an extension officer about drainage or raised beds before the next planting.
