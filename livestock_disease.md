# Livestock Disease and Sudden Sickness

Keywords: sick animal, livestock, cattle, goat, chicken, disease, not eating, diarrhea, sudden death

## Quick checks
Note which animals are affected, how many, and how fast it started. Many animals getting sick quickly points to something infectious, not an isolated injury.
Look for appetite loss together with fever, diarrhea, nasal discharge, or a sudden drop in milk or eggs.
Check for skin lesions, swelling, or lameness, which can point to specific diseases like foot and mouth or lumpy skin disease.

## What to do this week
Separate sick animals from the rest of the herd or flock right away to slow the spread.
Keep clean water and shade available for sick animals. Dehydration from diarrhea can kill faster than the disease itself.
Do not sell, slaughter, or move sick or dead animals off the farm. That risks spreading the disease to neighboring farms.
Disinfect shared feeding and watering equipment between healthy and sick animals if anything is on hand, even diluted bleach.
Write down symptoms and when they started, with photos if you can, so a veterinary officer has something to work from.

## When to get help
Any suspected outbreak, fast spread, multiple sudden deaths, or blistering around the mouth and feet, should be reported to the nearest veterinary office right away. Do not wait to see if it clears up on its own.
